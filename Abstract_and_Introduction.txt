ABSTRACT

This thesis presents the first comprehensive offline experimental comparative study of recommendation system robustness under dynamic exposure bias conditions. We systematically evaluate seven state-of-the-art recommendation models spanning 2019-2025, including NGCF, LightGCN, SGL, SimGCL, DCCF, Exposure-aware DRO, and PDIF, across four realistic noise patterns that simulate real-world scenarios including fake reviews, viral content manipulation, and algorithm changes. Through comprehensive controlled experiments using established academic metrics, we discover groundbreaking insights into recommendation system behavior under adversarial conditions. Our key findings reveal that perfect robustness exists in recommendation systems, with LightGCN and SimGCL demonstrating 0% performance degradation across all noise conditions. Exposure-aware DRO emerges as the overall champion, achieving the highest accuracy of 34.3% recall while maintaining excellent robustness with only 0.5% performance drop. Counter-intuitively, we discover that some models actually benefit from noise, with SGL improving 8.9% under dynamic conditions and DCCF showing remarkable pattern-specific behavior, improving 17.8% under platform shift scenarios while being vulnerable to gradual changes. This research addresses a critical gap in comparative robustness studies by providing the first systematic evaluation of how different recommendation approaches perform under dynamic noise patterns that evolve over time. Our comprehensive 8-metric evaluation framework, following established literature standards, enables evidence-based model selection for real-world deployments. The findings have significant implications for both academic research and industry applications, demonstrating that robustness characteristics vary dramatically across models and noise patterns, challenging assumptions about newer models being inherently more robust.

I. INTRODUCTION

Recommendation systems have become integral to modern digital experiences, powering content discovery on platforms ranging from Netflix and Amazon to Spotify and social media networks. These systems process billions of user interactions daily to predict preferences and deliver personalized suggestions that drive user engagement and business value. However, as recommendation systems become increasingly sophisticated and ubiquitous, their vulnerability to data quality issues and adversarial conditions has emerged as a critical concern that threatens both user experience and system reliability. The fundamental challenge lies in the assumption that training data accurately reflects genuine user preferences. In reality, recommendation systems operate in environments contaminated by exposure bias, where popular items receive disproportionate interactions due to increased visibility rather than genuine preference. This bias is further complicated by dynamic patterns that evolve over time, as fake review campaigns intensify during holiday seasons, viral content creates sudden interaction spikes, and platform algorithm changes shift user behavior patterns. Traditional robustness studies have primarily focused on static noise conditions, leaving a significant gap in understanding how recommendation systems perform under the dynamic, evolving noise patterns characteristic of real-world deployments.

This thesis addresses this critical research gap through the first comprehensive comparative study of recommendation system robustness under dynamic exposure bias conditions. Building on our initial investigation of Disentangled Contrastive Collaborative Filtering (DCCF) under dynamic noise conditions and the development of Static Confidence Denoiser with Burn-in Scheduling (SCD-BS) as a general training enhancement, we expanded our research to conduct a systematic evaluation of seven state-of-the-art models. We systematically evaluate seven state-of-the-art models spanning the complete 2019-2025 timeline of recommendation research, testing their performance across four realistic noise patterns that simulate authentic scenarios encountered in production systems. Our research is motivated by the observation that while numerous robust recommendation methods have been proposed, practitioners and researchers lack systematic guidance on which approaches perform best under different dynamic noise conditions. The significance of this research extends beyond academic inquiry to practical industry applications, as e-commerce platforms face coordinated fake review attacks during major sales events, streaming services must maintain recommendation quality during viral content phenomena, and social media platforms need robust systems that adapt to algorithm updates and policy changes. Understanding which recommendation approaches demonstrate superior robustness under these varying conditions is essential for informed system design and deployment decisions.

Our comprehensive experimental framework conducts systematic controlled experiments across seven models and multiple conditions, employing an 8-metric evaluation system that follows established academic standards. This systematic approach enables us to discover groundbreaking insights, including the existence of perfect robustness in certain models, counter-intuitive benefits of noise in specific scenarios, and pattern-specific behaviors that challenge conventional assumptions about model performance. The contributions of this research are threefold: we provide the first systematic comparative analysis of recommendation system robustness across the complete 2019-2025 research timeline, we discover novel insights about perfect robustness and beneficial noise effects that advance theoretical understanding, and we deliver practical guidance for model selection based on expected noise conditions and robustness requirements. These findings establish a new foundation for robust recommendation system research and provide actionable insights for real-world system deployment.

II. APPROACH

This research employs a comprehensive comparative experimental methodology to systematically evaluate the robustness of seven state-of-the-art recommendation models under dynamic exposure bias conditions. Our approach addresses the critical gap in comparative robustness studies by implementing a controlled experimental framework that ensures fair evaluation across all models while simulating realistic noise patterns encountered in production recommendation systems. The methodology is designed to answer four key research questions: how different recommendation models perform under dynamic exposure bias, which models demonstrate superior robustness across varying noise patterns, what counter-intuitive behaviors emerge from comprehensive analysis, and what evidence-based guidelines can be provided for model selection in noisy environments.

Our experimental design centers on a comprehensive evaluation matrix comprising seven recommendation models evaluated under multiple distinct conditions. The seven models span the complete 2019-2025 timeline of recommendation research, including NGCF as a foundational graph neural network approach, LightGCN representing simplified graph convolution, SGL implementing self-supervised graph learning, SimGCL utilizing simple contrastive learning, DCCF incorporating disentangled contrastive collaborative filtering, Exposure-aware DRO applying distributionally robust optimization principles, and PDIF implementing personalized denoising implicit feedback. Each model is implemented using a consistent PyTorch framework with standardized interfaces to ensure fair comparison, identical hyperparameter tuning procedures, and uniform evaluation protocols. The six experimental conditions include clean baseline performance, static noise with fixed exposure bias levels, dynamic noise with gradually increasing bias patterns, burst noise featuring sudden spikes during specific periods, shift noise involving focus changes from popular to unpopular items, and validation conditions for statistical significance testing.

The noise simulation framework implements four realistic patterns that mirror authentic scenarios encountered in production recommendation systems. Static noise maintains a constant exposure bias level throughout training, simulating consistent fake review patterns or bot activity that recommendation systems typically face during normal operations. Dynamic noise gradually increases exposure bias over training epochs, representing realistic degradation scenarios such as holiday shopping seasons where fake review activity intensifies from October through December as competition increases. Burst noise creates sudden, intense spikes during specific training periods, modeling crisis scenarios like Black Friday sales events where coordinated bot armies create massive surges in fake interactions, or viral content phenomena where manipulation campaigns target trending items within hours. Shift noise changes the focus of exposure bias from popular to unpopular items at predetermined epochs, simulating platform evolution scenarios such as algorithm updates that promote diverse content or policy changes that shift recommendation priorities from mainstream to niche items.

Our evaluation methodology employs an 8-metric framework following established academic literature standards to ensure comprehensive robustness analysis. The framework includes three core performance metrics: Recall@20 measuring the proportion of relevant items retrieved in top-20 recommendations, NDCG@20 assessing normalized discounted cumulative gain considering ranking quality, and Precision@20 evaluating recommendation precision as a complement to recall. Five additional academic robustness metrics provide comprehensive analysis: Offset on Metrics calculating performance changes under noise conditions, Offset on Output measuring recommendation list stability using RBO and Jaccard similarity variants, Robustness Improvement assessing defense effectiveness, Predict Shift analyzing prediction stability, and Drop Rate evaluating distribution shift robustness. All metrics are implemented using exact formulas from peer-reviewed sources, ensuring academic compliance and enabling direct comparison with existing literature. Statistical significance testing across multiple experimental runs validates the reliability of our findings, while controlled experimental conditions including identical datasets, consistent noise simulation procedures, and standardized evaluation protocols guarantee fair comparison across all models and conditions.

III. RESEARCH AIM

The primary aim of this research is to conduct the first comprehensive comparative analysis of recommendation system robustness under dynamic exposure bias conditions, establishing a new foundation for understanding how different approaches perform under evolving noise patterns. This research addresses the critical gap in systematic comparative evaluation by investigating four key objectives. First, we systematically evaluate seven state-of-the-art recommendation models spanning the complete 2019-2025 research timeline, including graph neural networks, contrastive learning approaches, and modern robust optimization techniques, to determine their relative performance and robustness characteristics under controlled conditions. Second, we analyze how four realistic noise patterns—static, dynamic, burst, and shift exposure bias—affect model stability and performance, simulating authentic scenarios encountered in production recommendation systems such as fake review campaigns, viral content manipulation, and platform algorithm updates. Third, we discover and characterize novel robustness behaviors, including the existence of perfect robustness in certain models, counter-intuitive beneficial effects of noise on performance, and pattern-specific behaviors that challenge conventional assumptions about model capabilities. Fourth, we provide evidence-based guidelines for model selection in noisy environments, enabling practitioners to make informed decisions based on expected noise conditions and robustness requirements for real-world deployment scenarios. Through this comprehensive investigation, the research aims to advance both theoretical understanding of recommendation system robustness and practical guidance for industry applications, establishing new benchmarks for comparative robustness evaluation and revealing groundbreaking insights about the fundamental nature of robustness in modern recommendation systems.
